# v2: 12 training kits, same 8 held-out kits

Run 2026-09-15 (scripts/run_12kit_chain.sh, 02:00 to 07:31). One seed each:
runs/drumjepa_v2_12k (aux_rec 0) and runs/drumjepa_v2_12k_auxrec01 (aux_rec 0.1), the
v1 recipe on configs/kits_v2.yaml and data/cache/v2.

## Question

Every held-out number in v1 is worse than its train-kit number: the action target's
E5 onset F1 is 0.58-0.66 on train kits and 0.25-0.40 held out, and the readout ratio
is lower held out too (docs/followups/item2.md, item5.md). One explanation is that 6
training kits are too few for the encoder to learn anything kit-general. v2 doubles
the training kits to 12 and keeps v1's 8 held-out kits, so held-out audio is identical
and the only thing that moves is training-kit diversity. configs/kits_v2.yaml records
how the six new kits were chosen and flags the one near-neighbour pair added
(Super Boom (Layered) in train, Raw Dnb (Layered Hybrid) held out).

Pass: the held-out content margin closes, or at least shrinks, for the action target.

## What is comparable

data/cache/v2 has its own stats.json (scripts/cache_stats.py; mean -5.119, std
3.423 against v1's -5.198, 3.424), so losses, prediction errors and anything in
normalized mel units are NOT comparable to v1 (notes/decisions.md, "Mel
normalization"). Only scale-free quantities are compared across caches: E1 win
rates, E5 probe F1, the readout full-mask/ceiling ratio and kit accuracy. Train-kit
columns are on different kit sets (6 vs 12); the held-out columns are on identical
audio and are the comparison that matters. E2 was not run: there is no 12-kit
action-only baseline (see the chain script header).

## Verdict

**The held-out margin does not close.** Held-out content is 0.195 for the baseline and
0.316 for the action target, inside v1's 0.19-0.21 and 0.25-0.40, while train-kit
content rose from 0.22-0.27 to 0.295 and from 0.58-0.66 to 0.648. The held-out /
train ratio for the action target is 0.49 at 12 kits against 0.51 for v1's seed mean.
Doubling training-kit diversity bought train-kit content and nothing held out.

**Within the pair, option A holds at 12 kits.** aux_rec 0.1 beats 0 on content
(0.648 vs 0.295 train, 0.316 vs 0.195 held-out), on the readout ratio (1.006 vs
0.928) and on the readout ceiling (0.665 vs 0.393), with E1 kit swap identical.
The baseline's readout ratio, 0.928, is above v1's 0.80-0.88, so the prediction gap
between the two arms is narrower at 12 kits (0.08 against about 0.13 on the v1 means);
one seed, and the v1 baseline's seed spread is 0.09 wide, so this is a note, not a
finding.

**The near-neighbour pair went the wrong way.** The kit split flagged Super Boom
(Layered) as a near neighbour of held-out Raw Dnb (Layered Hybrid), with the worry
that it would inflate Raw Dnb's number for the wrong reason. Raw Dnb is instead the
worst held-out kit for the action target at 0.182, down from 0.327, and below raw
mel (0.209). Whatever the encoder learns from Super Boom does not transfer to the kit
it was picked as a neighbour of.

**Held-out embeddings moved further from the train-kit distribution.** The action
target is lower than v1 seed 0 on 7 of 8 held-out kits; v1 seed 0 was the best of its
three seeds, and 0.316 is inside the v1 range, so one seed cannot say 12 kits is worse
held out, only that it is not better. The linear velocity probe says more: fit on
train kits, it is at 18.9 / 16.9 MAE on train kits and 66.6 / 35.3 on held-out kits,
against 24.5 for predicting the class mean and about 20 held-out at v1. A linear map
that extrapolates that badly means the held-out embeddings occupy a region the
train-kit embeddings do not, more so at 12 kits than at 6. That points the same way as
the content result: more training kits make the representation more kit-specific,
not more general.

**Reading.** "Six kits is too few" is refuted at this scale. The held-out gap looks
like a property of the objective (masked prediction in the model's own teacher space,
with the kit as the dominant axis of that space, docs/e3/), not of the data. Closing
it would need a change to what the state is asked to be invariant to, not more kits.

## Caveats

One seed per arm. The v1 baseline's seed spread on the readout ratio is 0.09 and the
action target's held-out E5 spread is 0.14, so any v1-vs-v2 difference smaller than
those is noise. The within-pair ordering (0.1 beats 0 on every content and prediction
axis) matches all three v1 seed pairs and is the robust part.

E2 was not run at 12 kits (no action-only baseline on this cache). The E5 probe and
the readout share the pooled tokens the aux head trains on, as in the follow-ups.

The velocity probe's held-out failure was not seen at v1 and has one seed; it would
be worth a second seed before building on it.
